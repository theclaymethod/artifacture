The closest point on a line

A projection keeps the part of a vector that points along a chosen direction.

Start with a point v and a line through the origin. The shortest route from the point to the line meets it at a right angle. That meeting point is the projection of v onto the line.

Separate the two directions

The vector from the origin to v splits into a component along the line and a perpendicular remainder. Moving to any other point on the line makes the distance longer.

Find the amount along the line

For any nonzero direction vector u, the dot product measures how much v points along it. Dividing by u · u accounts for the length of the direction vector.

p = ((v · u) / (u · u)) u

Choose a direction
u = (1, 0), the horizontal axis.
Take the dot product
(4, 3) · (1, 0) = 4.
Find the projection
p = 4(1, 0) = (4, 0).
Check the remainder
v − p = (0, 3), which is perpendicular to (1, 0).
